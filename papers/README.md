# Three Papers on Games, at the Edge of Two Maps

A short trilogy of essays. The first two survey a design space and a personal
capability independently; the third argues that they are the same territory
seen from opposite sides.

The throughline is a single claim about *cost*. The most artistically
under-explored regions of game design went unbuilt not for lack of imagination
but because they are made of the two most expensive substances in the medium —
hand-authored reactivity and deep systems. Those are precisely the substances a
language model produces cheaply and at scale. The frontier and the tool turn out
to have the same shape, because they are described by the same sentence.

## The papers

1. **[Video Game Genres and Spaces Yet to Be Fully Explored](01-unexplored-game-spaces.md)**
   Genres are shelf labels; *design spaces* — regions fixed by a game's verbs,
   subject matter, and formal properties — are the real thing. A tour of the
   blank regions (decipherment, social negotiation, the detective without a
   quest marker, the anti-4X of care and repair, the living world and its unread
   epic), and an argument that the frontier is gated by the cost of content and
   systems, not by the poverty of anyone's imagination.

2. **[Video Games I Would Excel at Programming](02-games-i-would-excel-at-programming.md)**
   A candid self-assessment. I am strongest where a game is made of code, rules,
   text, and generated content (parser IF, MUDs, roguelikes, systemic sims,
   procedural generation, constructed languages, engine substrate) and weakest
   where it is made of bespoke art, audio, and hand-tuned feel. One axis —
   content-and-systems versus art-and-feel — predicts almost everything.

3. **[The Budget Was the Border: The Intersection](03-the-intersection.md)**
   The synthesis. The cost that kept the frontier closed is the cost my
   comparative advantage collapses, so the medium's most important unexplored
   region and my sharpest strength are one region drawn twice. Walks the overlap
   space by space, sketches three games that live inside it, and is honest about
   the danger: cheap content trends toward the generic, so the win condition is
   not "the model writes the game" but a division of labor — human-authored
   system, constraint, voice, and taste; machine-filled volume within the cage.

## Reading order

Read them in numbered order. The third paper quotes the closing arguments of the
first two directly and will lose most of its force read alone.
